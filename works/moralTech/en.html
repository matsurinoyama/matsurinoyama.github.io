Introduction

Technologies Are Not Neutral Instruments

Technologies do much more than just perform their intended function. They give shape to what we do and how we experience the world. The same tools inadvertently creates brand new behaviours nobody accounted for.

Automobiles
They don't just move us from A→B. They lengthen our social radius, decide how far we live from work, and reshape how we design cities and roads.

Mobile Phones

The challenge
Mainstream ethics says morality is an exclusively human affair. Objects have no mind, free will, or intention, so crediting them with morality sounds like "animism." Yet things clearly shape our moral decisions. So one must ask:
How can designers anticipate, or even "build in," the moral roles of what they make?

Why current ethics falls short

The "Externalist" Trap

Even modern fields (engineering ethics, computer ethics) still separate two realms: protect "society" from the risks of "technology," and blow the whistle when tech misbehaves. Technology is treated as a mere instrument.

Verbeek's counter-claim
"Humans are technological beings, just as technologies are social entities."
The two domains cannot be cleanly separated; they constitute each other.
Mediation has a moral charge

Two Faces of Moral Mediation

Both cases show technology making moral decisions on our behalf. One by exclusion, one by transforming how we understand illness and responsibility.

Robert Moses' low overpasses
Langdon Winner's example: parkway overpasses to Long Island's beaches were deliberately built too low for buses to pass. This quietly restricted beach access for poorer, mostly African-American residents who relied on buses.
flyover
BRCA genetic testing
Tests for breast-cancer genes turn healthy people into potential patients and create a new category: the "not-yet-ill." The technology organizes a hard situation of choice, and even suggests which choice to make.
biotech

How Technology Transforms What We Perceive

Why it matters morally
Multistability
The same device has different identities by context: the telephone and typewriter began as aids for the deaf and blind.
By shaping what counts as "real" (think MRI or ultrasound imaging), technology shapes the decisions we make about disease, pregnancy, and an unborn child.
Embodiment relation
Tech is "incorporated" and becomes an extension of the body, like glasses you see through. (≈ ready-to-hand)
eyeglasses_2
Hermeneutic relation
Tech gives a representation that must be read. A thermometer shows a value to interpret, not heat you feel directly.
device_thermostat
Amplification & reduction
Viewing a tree through an infrared camera hides what the eye sees, but reveals something new: whether it is healthy.

Mediation of praxis · Bruno Latour

How Technology Shapes What We Do

Latour shows that artifacts carry a script. That is to say, they prescribe, invite, and inhibit actions. A gun translates "express my anger" into "kill that person." A speed bump slows you whether you agree or not. Action is mediated from both ready-to-hand and present-at-hand positions.

Script
Like a film script, an artifact prescribes how to act. A speed bump says "Slow down," a paper cup says "Throw me away."
assignment
Translation of action
"Prepare meals quickly" + a microwave → "regularly eat instant meals, alone." New composite goals emerge.
restaurant
Invitation & inhibition
Scripts invite some actions and discourage others, and this works materially, not as a sign to read.
